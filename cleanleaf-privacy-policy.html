1Who we are

CleanLeaf is an app for Android, published on Google Play by DuruSoft, based in Türkiye (“we”, “us”). For the limited personal data described here that we decide about, we are the data controller. Google acts as a separate controller or as our processor for the Google services below, and RevenueCat as our processor for purchases. You can reach us at duru.sofft@gmail.com.

3What stays on your device

CleanLeaf keeps the following in its private storage on your phone. We cannot access it.

  • Scan results: which photos and videos were checked, their size, date and folder, and the groups the scan found. Images are looked at on the device to measure sharpness and similarity; no image or part of one is stored outside your gallery.
  • Your choices: what you selected or kept, excluded folders, theme and other settings, notification choices.
  • Trash records and statistics: what you moved to the trash and when, and how much space each cleanup freed.
  • Contact backups: a copy of contacts made before a merge or delete, so you can undo it. Up to 10 automatic backups are kept.
  • Private vault: photos and videos you move there are encrypted with a key that stays on your device. Your PIN and recovery code are never sent anywhere.
  • Premium status: your last known plan, so the app works offline.

Uninstalling removes this data, including the private vault. Export what you want to keep from the vault before you uninstall.

4Permissions and why

Every permission is asked for only when you open the feature that needs it, after a short explanation in the app. You can refuse or withdraw any of them in Android Settings; the rest of the app keeps working.

  • Photos and videos: to scan your gallery and move what you choose to the trash.
  • All files access (some versions only): to show your storage and find large, duplicate and leftover files.
  • Delete without asking (media management, Android 12 and later, optional): so Android does not ask again for every cleanup you already confirmed in the app.
  • Contacts: to find and merge duplicate contacts and delete the ones you choose.
  • Usage access: to show app sizes and when each app was last used.
  • Request to uninstall apps: Android asks you to confirm each app you choose to remove.
  • Notifications (optional): a weekly summary, reminders you turn on, and progress while a long task runs.
  • Fingerprint or face unlock (optional): to open the private vault. Android handles the check; the app only learns whether it succeeded.
  • Internet: for ads, purchases and the update check.

5Advertising and your consent

On the free plan, CleanLeaf shows ads from Google AdMob. Ads never use your photos, files or contacts. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google’s consent form (User Messaging Platform) asks for your choice before any ad is requested; “Do not consent” is a valid answer and the app works the same. You can change your choice at any time in Me › Settings › Privacy & ads › Privacy options. Where required, US state privacy choices are offered the same way.

Google may collect the data listed in section 2 to show, measure and protect ads. See how Google uses data from apps that use its services. You can reset or delete your advertising ID in Android Settings › Privacy › Ads. With Premium, the ad service is never started and receives nothing.

6Purchases (Premium)

Premium is sold through Google Play; Google handles payment and your payment details. RevenueCat checks with Google Play whether you have Premium, using a random app user ID that is not linked to your name or email. It receives the purchase information listed in section 2. See RevenueCat’s privacy policy.

7Update check

When the app starts, it may read a small public file from our website (hosted by GitHub Pages) to learn the latest version and a few settings such as the daily free limit. GitHub sees your IP address, as with any web request. No data about you is sent with it.

8Device backup

If Android backup is on, only your CleanLeaf settings are included (for example theme and excluded folders). Scan results, statistics, trash records, contact backups and the private vault are never included.

9Crash reports

We do not use a crash-reporting service. If you allow your device to share usage and diagnostics, Google Play may send us anonymous crash and “not responding” reports.

10When you contact us

If you write to us (for example from Me › Settings › Support), we receive your email address and what you write. The app pre-fills the app and Android version, device model, language, your plan and whether photo access is allowed, to help us; you can remove them before sending. We use this only to answer you and fix the issue you report, and delete it when no longer needed, at the latest two years after our last exchange.

11Sharing and selling

We do not sell or rent your personal data. Data reaches only the services named in this policy, for the purposes stated. We may disclose information if the law requires it.

12Retention and deletion

Data on your device stays until you clear the app’s data or uninstall it. Items you move to the trash are deleted for good by Android after 30 days, or earlier if you empty the trash. Google and RevenueCat keep data according to their own policies. How to delete everything: Delete your data.

13Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or restrict the use of your personal data, to object to its use, and to withdraw consent. Because we hold almost nothing about you, most of these are done on your device: clear or uninstall the app, change ad choices in the app, or reset your advertising ID. For data held by Google or RevenueCat, write to us and we will help. You can also complain to your data protection authority.

14Children

CleanLeaf is meant for adults and is not directed at children under 13 (or the minimum age in your country). We do not knowingly collect data from children.

15Security

Your media and contacts are processed on your device. The private vault is encrypted on the device, and connections to Google and RevenueCat use encryption in transit. No method is perfect, but we keep what leaves your phone to the minimum.

16International transfers

Google and RevenueCat may process data in other countries, including the United States, under safeguards such as standard contractual clauses.

17Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we update the effective date above, and for important changes we tell you in the app.
