3What stays on your phone

The following is processed and stored only on your phone. We have no server that receives it, and we cannot see it.

  • Statuses you view. The app reads statuses from the WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business status folder that you choose in Android’s own folder picker. It gets read-only access to that folder; on Android 10 and later it reads no other folder. It also keeps a small record of which statuses it has already shown or saved, so that auto-save copies each status only once.
  • Copies you save, and auto-save. Saved copies go to your gallery: Pictures/StatusSaver, Movies/StatusSaver (when separate folders are on) and Music/StatusSaver for voice statuses. Auto-save, if you turn it on, copies new statuses there about every 15 minutes and when you open the app. These copies are in your phone’s shared storage, so your gallery, other apps you allowed to see your photos, and any photo backup you use (for example Google Photos) can see them. Uninstalling the app does not delete them.
  • The vault. Files you move to the vault are kept in the app’s private storage and hidden from the gallery. The vault opens with your phone’s screen lock, fingerprint or face (Android checks it; the app only learns whether it succeeded), or with a 4-digit vault PIN if your phone has no screen lock. The PIN is stored only as a salted, derived value, never in plain form, and it cannot be recovered. The vault locks when you leave it, and screenshots of it are blocked. Vault files have no other copy.
  • Kept messages from WhatsApp notifications, only if you turn the feature on. See section 4.
  • Stickers. The photos you pick, the cut-outs and the sticker packs you make are kept in the app’s private storage. When you add a pack to WhatsApp, WhatsApp reads that pack from the app. Only apps holding WhatsApp’s sticker permission can read packs, and only packs you have sent.
  • Status Studio, Enhance and the Video splitter. The images you design, the photos you sharpen and brighten, and the parts the splitter cuts are made on your phone and saved to the StatusSaver album in your gallery. Photos and videos you pick come through Android’s photo picker, and the app receives only what you picked.
  • Direct chat. The last five numbers you opened are kept on your phone so you can open them again. The app does not read your contacts. When you tap “Open chat”, the number and the optional message are passed to WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business as a wa.me link.
  • Settings and progress: your choices in Profile, your streak, milestones and Sparks, daily free-use counters, and your last known plan so Premium works offline.
  • Widget and Quick Settings tile. If you add the widget, it shows your new statuses on your home screen, where anyone holding your phone can see them. The Quick Settings tile turns auto-save on or off.

Backups and new phones

The app’s own data is never included in Android’s cloud backup. On Android 12 and later, if you move to a new phone with a direct phone-to-phone transfer, vault files, sticker packs, recent Direct chat numbers, settings and other app data can be moved; kept messages, their photos and the vault PIN are never moved. On the new phone you open the vault with its screen lock or a new PIN.

Deleting what is on your phone

You can delete saved copies, vault files, sticker packs and kept messages in the app. Clearing the app’s storage in Android settings, or uninstalling the app, deletes everything in its private storage (vault, kept messages, stickers, recent numbers and settings). Copies in your gallery stay until you delete them there.

4Deleted messages and notification access

The “Deleted messages” feature keeps a copy of WhatsApp messages that your phone shows as notifications, so you can still read one if the sender deletes it later. It cannot bring back messages from WhatsApp itself.

  • Off until you turn it on. Before anything is read, the app explains what it reads, where it is kept and how to stop. You then turn on notification access for Status Saver in Android’s settings.
  • What is read. Android gives an app with notification access the notifications of all apps. Status Saver ignores every notification that does not come from WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business; those are not read or stored. From WhatsApp and WhatsApp Business notifications it keeps the sender’s name, the message text and the time. With Premium, it also keeps a photo preview (at most 1080 pixels) when the notification shows one. Group summaries, calls and service notifications are skipped.
  • Where it is kept. Only in the app’s private database on your phone. Kept messages are never sent over the network, never included in usage statistics, and never moved to a new phone or into a cloud backup.
  • How long. Free version: the last 24 hours; older messages are deleted automatically. Premium: until you clear them. If Premium ends, messages older than 24 hours are deleted at the next cleanup.
  • How to stop. Use “Turn off access” in the feature’s menu, or turn off notification access in Android’s settings, at any time. Messages kept before stay readable until you delete them (on the free version, for up to 24 hours). “Clear all” deletes every kept message and photo preview; this cannot be undone.

Kept messages contain other people’s names and words. Please treat them with care and respect the privacy of the people who write to you.

5Permissions and why

Permissions that need your OK are asked for only when you use the feature that needs them. You can refuse or withdraw them in Android settings; the rest of the app keeps working.

  • Folder access (Android’s folder picker): read-only access to the WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business status folder you choose. This is not a storage permission, and you can choose the folder again or remove access at any time.
  • Storage, only on Android 8 and 9: “read and write external storage”, to save copies to your gallery on these older versions. On Android 10 and later no storage permission is requested.
  • Notification access (optional): only for “Deleted messages”, as described in section 4.
  • Notifications (Android 13 and later, optional): a reminder before a free trial ends, and, if you turn it on, at most one quiet auto-save summary a day.
  • Fingerprint or face unlock (optional): to open the vault. Android checks your fingerprint, face or screen lock; the app only learns whether the check succeeded.
  • Internet and network state: for ads, purchases, usage statistics, crash reports, app settings and downloading the sticker cut-out tool. Viewing and saving statuses work offline.
  • Google Play billing: to buy Premium.
  • Advertising ID and Android’s ad services (advertising ID, ad attribution and ad topics, where your Android version supports them): used by Google’s ads software on the free version, subject to your consent choices.
  • Keep the phone awake (wake lock): lets background work such as auto-save finish.
  • Seeing whether WhatsApp is installed: the app checks only for WhatsApp and WhatsApp Business, to find their status folders, open Direct chat and add sticker packs. It does not list the other apps on your phone.

Status Saver does not request permission to read your contacts, your location, your camera or microphone, or all your photos and videos. On Android 8 and 9 only, it asks for the storage permission to save copies to your gallery.

6Advertising and your consent

The free version shows ads from Google AdMob: banners, ads in lists, a full-screen ad at some natural breaks, an ad when you come back to the app after a while, and, only if you choose it, a short video ad that gives you Sparks or two hours without ads. We ask Google to show only ads rated for a general audience with parental guidance (“PG”) or lower. Ads never use your statuses, saved files or messages.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, and wherever else the law requires it, Google’s consent form (User Messaging Platform) asks for your choice before ads are requested. Refusing is a valid answer; the app keeps working, and Google may then show non-personalized or limited ads. Where the rules apply to you, you can change your choice at any time in Profile › Ad privacy choices. In US states with privacy laws, the same place lets you opt out of the “sale” or “sharing” of your data for personalized ads, where the law gives you that right.

Türkiye. Personalized ads are shown only if you allow them. After the setup, the app asks you once, on its own screen with two equal buttons, whether Google may use your advertising ID and how you use the app to choose ads by your interests. Until you answer, and if you refuse, every ad is requested as non-personalized; the app works the same either way. You can change your answer at any time in Profile › Personalised ads; the change applies at once. Your answer, when you gave it and which text you saw are stored on your phone. Non-personalized ads may still use the advertising ID for frequency capping, fraud prevention and aggregate reporting. The notice in Turkish is in section 21.

With Premium, no ad is requested and the ads software is not initialized. Google’s consent tool still runs at start and, where the law requires it and you have not answered yet, may show its form. To limit ads tracking on any app, you can reset or delete your advertising ID in Android settings (on most phones Settings › Privacy › Ads, or Settings › Google › Ads).

Google uses the data listed in section 2 under its own policies. See how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy.

7Purchases

Premium (weekly or yearly subscription, or a one-time lifetime purchase) is sold through Google Play. Google handles the payment and your payment details; we never see them. RevenueCat checks with Google Play what you bought and tells the app which plan you have. It uses a random app user ID that is not linked to your name or email, and receives the purchase information listed in section 2.

If you bought “remove ads” in an earlier version of the app, the app asks Google Play for that purchase once, through RevenueCat, so that it becomes Premium for life without you doing anything.

So that a purchase can come back after you reinstall the app or move to a new phone, RevenueCat may store its random ID with Google Play services on your phone, which can include it in your Google device backup. It contains no name, email or file. See RevenueCat’s privacy policy.

The free-trial reminder is a notification scheduled on your phone; nothing is sent to us for it.

8Usage statistics, crash reports and app settings

Usage statistics (Google Firebase Analytics). To learn which features help people and where the app gets in the way, the app records events linked only to a random app instance ID (not to your name, phone number or account), such as opening a screen, saving a status, using a tool, a step in a purchase or an ad being shown. The app is built so that these events can never contain a file name, folder, link, phone number, email, message, caption, contact or other free text.

Crash reports (Google Firebase Crashlytics). If the app crashes, a report of what went wrong in the code is sent with the technical details listed in section 2.

Your switch. Both are on by default and both stop when you turn off Profile › Share usage statistics. When it is off, nothing is recorded or sent. You can turn it back on at any time.

App settings (Google Firebase Remote Config). The app downloads its settings from Google, for example which features are on, how often ads may appear and which offers are shown. This uses a Firebase installation ID and basic device information, and it is needed for the app to run.

If you allow your phone to share usage and diagnostics data with app developers, Google Play may also send us crash and performance reports that do not identify you. See the Firebase privacy information.

9Stickers and Google ML Kit

The sticker maker cuts the subject out of a photo with Google ML Kit’s subject segmentation. It runs on your phone: the photo is never sent to Google or to us. Google Play services downloads the cut-out tool once; after that it works without a connection. If the tool is not available, you can use the whole photo instead.

To provide the tool, Google receives the diagnostic data listed in section 2. According to Google, ML Kit uses it for diagnostics and usage analytics. See Google’s ML Kit data disclosure.

We use this only to answer you and fix the issue you report, and we delete it when it is no longer needed, at the latest two years after our last exchange.

12Legal bases

14How long data is kept

  • On your phone: until you delete it, clear the app’s storage or uninstall the app. Kept messages on the free version: 24 hours.
  • Usage statistics: event data is kept by Google Firebase for the period set in our Analytics settings, at most 14 months, then deleted.
  • Crash reports: Google keeps them for up to 90 days.
  • Purchase records (RevenueCat, Google Play): as long as needed to provide Premium and as long as tax, accounting or consumer laws require; you can ask us to delete RevenueCat’s record (section 15).
  • Advertising data: Google keeps it under its own policies.
  • Emails to us: at the latest two years after our last exchange.

15Your rights and how to use them

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to know what personal data we hold, to get a copy, to correct or delete it, to restrict or object to its use, to receive it in a portable form, to withdraw consent at any time (without affecting what happened before), and to opt out of the sale or sharing of personal data or of personalized ads. In Türkiye you have the rights in KVKK Article 11. We will not treat you differently for using these rights.

How to use them

  • Data on your phone: delete it in the app, clear the app’s storage, or uninstall the app (section 3).
  • Ads: Profile › Ad privacy choices (where shown), delete or reset your advertising ID in Android settings, or choose Premium.
  • Usage statistics and crash reports: turn off Profile › Share usage statistics.
  • Kept messages: “Turn off access” and “Clear all” in “Deleted messages”.
  • Data held by RevenueCat or Google Firebase: email duru.sofft@gmail.com. These records use random IDs, not your name, so we may ask for something that helps us find them, such as your Google Play order number (it starts with “GPA.”) or the approximate date of a purchase. We ask only for what we need.

We answer within one month (GDPR), 30 days (KVKK) or 45 days (US state laws), and tell you if we need more time where the law allows. If you are in a US state that gives you the right to appeal our decision, reply to our answer and we will review it. You may also use an authorized agent where your law allows. You can complain to your data protection authority; in Türkiye, after applying to us, to the Personal Data Protection Board (Kişisel Verileri Koruma Kurulu).

16Children

Status Saver is not directed to children under 13, or the higher minimum age that applies in your country. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children. If you believe a child has used the app and data about them reached RevenueCat or Firebase, write to us and we will delete it.

17Security

Your statuses, files, vault and kept messages are processed on your phone. The vault and kept messages live in the app’s private storage, which other apps cannot read; the vault is locked with your screen lock or a PIN stored only as a salted, derived value; screenshots of the vault are blocked; and the app’s data is left out of cloud backups. Connections to Google and RevenueCat are encrypted in transit. No method is perfect, so we keep what leaves your phone to the minimum.

18Countries without Google Play billing or ads

Where Google Play does not take payments for the app or Google does not serve ads (currently Russia, mainland China, Afghanistan and Iran), every Premium feature is free and no ad is requested. The app decides this on your phone from your SIM card’s country, or your phone’s region if there is no SIM.
